# Kernel design notes

Decisions behind the prototype's kernel and elaborator, and the gaps they leave. The full design,
including the Lean bridge and the compiled kernel, is
[docs/research/TypeTheoryEngine.html](../research/TypeTheoryEngine.html).

## Representation

Core terms are locally nameless. Bound variables are de Bruijn indices, free variables are integers
naming entries of a local context (`<|id -> <|"Name", "Type", "Binder"|>|>`), and going under a binder
opens it with a fresh free variable. The kernel never handles loose indices, and alpha-equivalent terms
are identical expressions, so `SameQ` and `Association` keys compare them structurally.

Every name is held, `HoldComplete[name]`, in binders and in constant references, because the prelude's
constants are built-in symbols (`Equal`, `Plus`) and user names may have values.

The composite heads, `application`, `lambdaAbstraction`, `dependentFunctionType`, `localDefinition`,
`projection` and `cubicalSystem`, are `HoldAllComplete`, so evaluating a term stops at its head, and every
construction computes its parts first. The kernel's steps keep their locals in `Block` and dispatch on
their definitions' patterns, never substituting into a term. Wolfram 15's evaluator would otherwise walk a
term as the tree it writes out to, which doubles with each integer added: `EvaluationCosts.md`.

A universe level is normalized to a maximum of components, each an integer or a base with an offset.
`levelEquivalentQ` compares normal forms. `levelLessEqualQ` is sound but incomplete: it can reject a
true inequality between levels with parameters, which would reject a valid declaration, never accept a
false one.

## The typing rules

The kernel's rules are those of a pure type system with Lean's universes. The table names each rule as R. Nederpelt and
H. Geuvers name it in *Type Theory and Formal Proof* (Cambridge, 2014), and the function of `TypeChecking.wl` that
applies it.

| Rule | Judgment | Kernel |
|---|---|---|
| (sort) | `Sort u : Sort (u + 1)` | `inferTypeOf[_, _, universeSort[u]]` |
| (var) | `x : A` in Γ gives Γ ⊢ `x : A` | `inferTypeOf[_, _, freeVariable[id]]` |
| (form) | Γ ⊢ `A : Sort u` and Γ, `x : A` ⊢ `B : Sort v` give Γ ⊢ `∀x:A. B : Sort (imax u v)` | `functionTypeSort` |
| (abst) | Γ, `x : A` ⊢ `b : B`, with `A` a type, gives Γ ⊢ `λx:A. b : ∀x:A. B` | `lambdaType` |
| (appl) | Γ ⊢ `f : ∀x:A. B` and Γ ⊢ `a : A` give Γ ⊢ `f a : B[x := a]` | `spineType`, `spineTypeFrom` |
| (conv) | Γ ⊢ `a : A` and `A ≡ B` give Γ ⊢ `a : B` | `checkInferredType`, `definitionallyEqualQ` |
| (def), (inst) | a constant `c : T` with universe parameters is `c.{levels} : T[levels]`, and unfolds by δ | `constantType`, `reduceConstant` |
| (let) | Γ ⊢ `v : A` and Γ, `x : A := v` ⊢ `b : B` give Γ ⊢ `(let x : A := v; b) : B[x := v]` | `localDefinitionType` |

An application is typed as a spine: `f a1 ... an` instantiates the codomain of each binder with the arguments before
it, all at once. A projection, a quotient's constants and the cubical primitives have rules of their own
(`projectionType`, `Quotients.wl`, `Cubical.wl`), and the constants of an inductive type are generated by
`InductiveTypes.wl`.

`imax u 0` is 0, so a product into a proposition is a proposition whatever its domain: Prop is impredicative. With the
rules `"CalculusOfInductiveConstructions"` the system is (sorts `Sort u`, axioms `Sort u : Sort (u + 1)`, rules
`(Sort u, Sort v, Sort (imax u v))`). With `"MartinLofTypeTheory"`, which has no Prop, every level is at least 1 and
`imax` is `max`: the system is predicative. Neither has cumulativity, as Lean has none.

`Checking/TypeChecking.wlt` checks the rules against the judgments of the book, as José Martín-García worked them in
his notebook *Calculus of Inductive Constructions* (WRI, 2015-2017). They include the Church booleans in Prop, whose
negation instantiates their quantifier at the booleans themselves and is refused over Type, the impredicative
encodings of chapter 7, and the dependent spines of chapter 5. `Checking/Queries.wlt` forges records the elaborator
would refuse, as a binder whose domain is no type, and the kernel refuses each one itself.

## The kernel

`inferType` checks: an application checks its argument, a binder checks that its domain is a type, a
local definition checks its value. Proof irrelevance, K-like reduction, η for structures and unit types
ask types too (`typeQuery`). Inside a declaration's check they infer them without checking, as Lean's
kernel does in infer-only mode: arguments are not checked against their binders, nor domains as types,
nor local values against their types. That is sound only where every term compared is checked or a
reduct of a checked term. A declaration's check is such a place, since it checks the type before the
value and each subterm before comparing the types of its parts. `withCheckedTerms` marks such a place:
the declarations, inductive blocks, quotients and cubical primitives, and `DefinitionallyEqualQ`, whose
terms are checked as their elaboration finishes. Elsewhere, in the elaborator and in tactics, the
queries check. A type inferred without checking goes to a cache of its own, so a check never takes it
for a checked one. Every call of `definitionallyEqualQ` outside `TypeChecking.wl` says in a comment
which of the two its terms are. A test in `Queries.wlt` forges theorems whose ill-typed proof is reached
only through proof irrelevance, and the declaration's own check catches each one.

Reduction is β, ζ, δ for definitions (theorems and opaque definitions are never unfolded), ι, K-like ι,
and the registered operations on natural number literals, on integers; the registered addition reduces by its two
defining equations, its recursion folded (Natural numbers, below). Definitional equality decides in
Lean's order. First come `SameQ`, sorts, literals and binders, then the offset rule for natural numbers. Then each side is reduced without
unfolding a definition, and the reducts are compared again if either changed. Where neither changes,
proof irrelevance answers for proofs: True for proofs of one proposition, False for proofs of two
different ones. Otherwise Lean's lazy delta reduction runs, and finally η, literals against
constructors, spines, η for structures and unit types. Lazy delta reduction unfolds one definition at a
time. A projection function, a definition whose value projects a field out of its last argument, is an
abbreviation (its record says `"Hint" -> "Abbreviation"`), as Lean gives its structure projections
abbreviation hints. An abbreviation unfolds before a regular definition, and two abbreviations unfold
together. Of two regular definitions the higher one unfolds, where a definition's height is one more
than the greatest height of the definitions its value mentions, or both at the same height. With the
abbreviations, the root of `IntermediateField.Lifts.le_union` checks in 14.5 s, against 170 s by
heights alone and 25 ms in Lean: `x.toRingHom` against a long composition reads the field before the
composition unfolds. As in Lean's kernel, lazy delta reduction is a loop that compares the sides
between steps only by identity, and by their arguments where one regular definition is at both heads,
from the first pair on. It stops when neither side has a definition at its head, and that last pair is
compared in full. Proof irrelevance is not asked again between steps, since unfolding leaves a proof a
proof of the same proposition. The kernel remembers inferred types, weak head normal forms and
definitional equalities for the length of a check.

A kernel failure is `Throw[Failure[tag, ...], $kernelFailureTag]`. Every exported function catches it
with `catchKernelFailure`, so a user sees a `Failure` and never a message.

## Inductive families

`declareInductiveTypes` follows Lean 4's kernel, for a single family, a block of mutual ones and families nested in
another inductive type:

- the type is a telescope of parameters and indices ending in a sort;
- each constructor takes the parameters first, with the same types, and ends in the type applied to
  those parameters;
- the type occurs strictly positively, and never in an index of a constructor's result;
- fields live in universes no larger than the type's, unless the type is a proposition.

The recursor eliminates into any universe unless the type may be a proposition and has more than one
constructor, or a field that is neither a proof nor an index of the result. A proposition with one
constructor and no fields, such as `Equal`, gets K-like reduction. The recursor type and its reduction
rules are built with free variables and abstracted at the end, and both are type-checked before they
are added.

The types of a mutual block share their parameters and their universe, and each constructor ends in one of them. A
family nested in the parameters of another inductive type `J`, as a tree whose children are a list of trees, is
checked as Lean's kernel checks it: each such occurrence `J ps` becomes an auxiliary type of the block, whose
constructors are `J`'s for the parameters `ps`, so the block is checked as a mutual one. Its recursors are generated
with the auxiliary types, and then `J ps` takes each one's place again. An auxiliary type's recursor is
`InductiveRecursor[I, k]`, as Lean names it `I.rec_k`.

## Natural numbers

`registerNaturalNumbers` checks the shape of the type and its two constructors, and the type of each operation
registered for a role. An operation with defining equations, addition, multiplication, power, subtraction, equality
and order, must satisfy them by computation for variables `t` and `s`, as the prelude's definitions do; an addition by
recursion on its first argument computes the literals right and fails the second of its equations. A quotient and a
remainder are checked against theirs, by recursion on the dividend, and trusted where they fail them, as Lean's, defined
by well-founded recursion, do. An operation without equations, as the greatest common divisor, is trusted to compute on literals what its role's function computes on integers,
as Lean's kernel trusts GMP, and `TypeTheoryAxiomClosure` names its registration as a trusted assumption wherever a proof
rests on it: a projection registered as the greatest common divisor proves `False`, as the tutorial *Paradoxes* shows.
Where the compiled kernel checks an environment or a list of declarations, it also checks these equations, and the
registration does not compare them again. What is trusted now that was not: on success the comparison by the kernel of
TypeChecking.wl no longer runs for these equations, so the compiled kernel's check of a record is the comparison, made
once where it was made twice. An equation `l == r` in `t` and `s` goes to it as a theorem's record after
the environment's constants: the statement that every predicate that holds of `l` holds of `r`,
`ForAll[{t, s, P}, P[l] -> P[r]]`, with the function that gives its hypothesis back for a proof, which checks exactly
where `l` and `r` are equal by computation. Only the equations with a variable in every application of a registered
operation go: the compiled kernel has the roles of an environment for its whole run and computes a registered
operation where every argument is a literal, so the one closed equation, `equal[zero, zero] == true`, it would compute
by the registration the equation is to justify, and the registration compares that one itself, in an environment
without the registration. This is sound while the compiled kernel reduces an open application by unfolding alone: it
uses a role only on literals, and has no rule that reads a sum by its offset. The guard of those two facts is a test, not the comment that states them at the compiled kernel's role code:
"The-compiled-kernel-alone-refuses-a-different-function-for-each-role" offers a different function for each role with
the comparison left out, and the compiled kernel must refuse it; a compiled kernel that used a role on an open term
would accept one, and the test would fail. The comparison is left
out (`$deferRegistrationEquations`) in two places only, which a test of the sources holds
("The-comparison-of-a-registrations-equations-is-left-out-in-two-places-only"): while a list of declarations is added before the compiled
kernel checks it, the list being kept only where the check succeeds, and while an environment is declared again after
the compiled kernel has checked it. Where the compiled kernel refuses an equation, the registration's own comparison
runs and names it, so a failure reads as it did. The intended end state is a role that is active only from its
registration's place in the order, which needs no such condition and also settles the declarations between an
operation's definition and its registration, where the compiled kernel computes by the role and the kernel of
TypeChecking.wl unfolds; it goes with the next change of the compiled program, when recursors are generated there.

The addition's equations are what the kernel reads terms through:
- definitional equality compares `x + k` with `y + k'` by their offsets, as Lean's kernel does, in a loop;
- literal values are read by offsets, and a sum with a variable in it is no literal;
- the registered addition reduces by its equations, its recursion folded, so its offset stays readable.

[KernelLiterals.md](KernelLiterals.md) has the design, the measurements and the agreement test,
`scripts/offset_agreement.wls`.

The compiled kernel holds a natural number literal below 2^62. A larger one is no row of its input: `environmentInput`
writes it as a reference to a constant made for its value, one for each distinct value, declared as an axiom of the
natural numbers' type right after the natural numbers, their zero and their successor, under a name no constant of an
environment has (`largeLiteral[n]`); a literal the compiled kernel holds is never made a constant. What is trusted in
this: that a judgement about a constant with no rule holds of any closed term of its type put in its place, so that a
record the compiled kernel accepts with the constant is one the kernel of TypeChecking.wl accepts with the literal.
The compiled kernel can then only accept less. Where a record needs the value of such a literal, or a registered
operation's result on literals passes 2^62, which ends the check with `eLiteralOverflow` and never wraps, the record
is undecided, and only in an environment that has a large literal: `compiledDeclarations` has the kernel of
TypeChecking.wl check that record's declaration in the environment of the declarations before it, added as the batch
added them, and its verdict is the batch's; the compiled kernel then goes on with the record taken as it is. The
rules of a recursor are checked at the end of a pass, which a pass that ends at an undecided record does not reach, so
recursors stay checked from pass to pass. Elsewhere a record the compiled kernel refuses is refused by that, as
before. `TypeTheoryCheck` gives the compiled kernel no recheck yet, and leaves an environment with an undecided
record to the kernel of TypeChecking.wl whole: a cone of 207 constants around `USize.size` takes it 3.1 s, as long as
its declarations one at a time, where the batch takes 1.2 s (at background priority). Giving `TypeTheoryCheck` the
recheck of one record is the next step. The guards are tests, in Tests/Checking/CompiledKernel.wlt: the two
kernels give one verdict on batches with large literals, true and false; no literal wraps past the limit; the other
kernel is asked only where a large literal is present; and a recursor with a forged rule before an undecided record
is caught. The end state is big numbers in the compiled kernel itself, which goes with its next recompile, beside
the role that is active only from its registration's place.

## Environments

An environment is a value. Declaring returns a new environment; `$TypeTheoryEnvironment` is just the
default, which the `Declare` functions update on success. `TypeTheoryCheck[environment]` replays each
record in order with the core declaration functions and requires the regenerated records to be
identical. Constructors and recursors are regenerated with their inductive type, and the natural
numbers are registered again as soon as the constants they name exist. This is the defense against a
hand-written environment; the tests forge a theorem statement, a recursor and the addition.

## Elaboration

The elaborator is untrusted and its output is always re-checked. Its state is bound with `Block` for
one elaboration: metavariable types and assignments, level assignments, the local context and the scope
of names.

- A metavariable has no local context of its own. A solution that names a variable from inside a
  binder can therefore escape it. The two mitigations are filling solved metavariables into binder types
  before abstracting (`instantiateContext`) and the kernel's final check, which reports any escape that
  remains. Lean's delayed assignments are the real fix.
- Unification is first-order with a fallback to weak head normal form, plus higher-order patterns:
  `?f[x1, ..., xn] =?= t` with distinct context variables solves `?f` as a function.
- Assigning a metavariable unifies its type with its solution's type, which solves universe levels.
- In an application, an argument whose expected type has an unsolved metavariable at its head waits
  until the application's type has been unified with the expected type.

## Proof states

A goal is a metavariable of the proof state, with a context, a type and, after induction, the
constructor it came from. "Introduce" records its solution as `pendingAbstraction[context, ids, body]`,
which becomes a function only once `body` is complete. This is the prototype's stand-in for delayed
assignments. Goals may mention other goals; the elaborator sees their types through
`$goalMetavariableTypes`, and a solution it finds for one of them is kept.
